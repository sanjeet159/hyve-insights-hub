## The 3 pricing models — and when to use each

 
 MODEL 01

## When to switch to value-based pricing

If your work directly drives revenue, charge a fraction of the value created — not the hours spent. Example: a landing page that lifts conversions by 1% on a ₹1Cr/mo product = ₹12L/year in incremental revenue. Charging ₹2L for that page is a steal for the client and 4x your hourly rate.

 Rule of thumb

 Value pricing = 8–15% of the first-year financial value your work creates for the client.

## 5 pricing mistakes that keep you stuck

 
 01
 Quoting before understanding the project

Ask 5 questions minimum before any number leaves your mouth.

 

 
 02
 Charging the same as a fresher

3 years of experience deserves 2x the rate. Raise prices every 6–9 months.

 

 
 03
 Sending a single number quote

Always send 3 tiers (Basic / Recommended / Premium). Anchors clients to the middle.

 

 
 04
 Not asking for upfront / escrow

Always 25–30% upfront, milestone-released. No exceptions for new clients.

 

 
 05 ★
 Discounting without removing scope

If the client cannot pay full price, reduce the deliverables — never the rate.

 

## Frequently asked questions

 
 Should Indian freelancers charge in INR or USD?
 +
 
 
 Charge in the client's currency. Indian clients pay in INR via HYVE escrow. International clients pay in USD/EUR. Never give a discount because the client is Indian — price for value, not geography.
 

 
 How often should I raise my rates?
 +
 
 
 Every new client should get a slightly higher rate than the last. For existing retainer clients, a 10–15% increase every 9–12 months is standard and almost never refused.
 

 
 What do I do if a client says my price is too high?
 +
 
 
 Never drop the rate. Drop the scope. Offer a smaller version that fits their budget. This protects your hourly rate and lets the client upgrade later when they see results.
 

 
 Is hourly or fixed pricing better for new freelancers?
 +
 
 
 Fixed-price with milestone payments. New freelancers underestimate hours and lose money on hourly. Fixed pricing forces you to scope clearly and protects your earnings per project.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### How should Indian freelancers price their services in 2026?

Price on the value of the outcome, not hours. Set a project fee based on the client's result, keep a rate floor you never go below, and use retainers for ongoing work.

### What is a good hourly rate for freelancers in India?

Ranges vary widely: roughly Rs 500-1,200 for junior work, Rs 1,500-3,000 for experienced specialists, and higher for niche product, growth or AI expertise.

### Should I show my prices publicly?

Publish starting ranges to filter out low-budget enquiries, then quote precisely after a short scoping call so the price matches the actual scope.

### How do I raise my rates without losing clients?

Raise on new projects first, communicate increases with the results you delivered, and offer a retainer that gives the client predictability in exchange for the new rate.
